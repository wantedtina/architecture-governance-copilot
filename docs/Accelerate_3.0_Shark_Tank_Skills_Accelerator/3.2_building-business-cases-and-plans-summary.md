# Building Business Cases & Plans

## Collection Overview

This learning collection focuses on turning an idea or proposed
initiative into a credible business case, converting that case into an
executable plan, and then managing the resulting project through
planning, launch, execution, and communication.

The material spans three closely connected capabilities:

1.  **Business case and business planning** --- deciding whether an
    initiative is worth pursuing and building a convincing plan around
    it.
2.  **Project planning and delivery** --- translating the approved idea
    into scope, schedule, resources, risks, and day-to-day execution.
3.  **Communication and stakeholder buy-in** --- presenting information
    clearly enough to gain support and keep stakeholders aligned.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 1. Making the Case for Your Business Project

A strong business case helps decision-makers determine whether an
initiative deserves investment.

The course emphasizes that a business case should go beyond describing
an idea. It should provide the information needed to make a decision,
including:

-   the initiative's objectives;
-   expected costs and benefits;
-   potential risks;
-   possible alternatives or solutions;
-   evidence and analysis supporting the recommendation;
-   criteria for evaluating whether the initiative succeeds.

The central idea is that a business case should make the rationale for
an initiative explicit and allow leaders to compare its expected value
with its cost and risk.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 2. Preparing and Implementing a Business Plan

A business plan provides a structured path from an idea to execution.

The course covers:

-   what a business plan is and why it matters;
-   the business-planning process;
-   internal analysis;
-   external and SWOT analysis;
-   creating action plans;
-   determining how implementation should be measured, monitored, and
    assessed.

The key distinction is that a business case helps answer **"Should we do
this?"**, while a business plan increasingly addresses **"How will we
make this work?"**

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 3. Business Acumen: Planning

Planning is presented as a systematic management discipline rather than
simply creating a schedule.

Important elements include:

-   setting goals;
-   formulating strategies;
-   allocating resources;
-   making decisions that support long-term success;
-   understanding how plans are created and executed;
-   defining appropriate performance measures;
-   using KPIs to monitor progress and determine whether the plan is
    succeeding.

The course connects strategic planning with measurable execution: a plan
needs both a clear direction and a way to determine whether the
organization is actually moving toward it.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Project Management

Once an initiative has been justified and planned at the business level,
the collection shifts toward executing it as a project.

## 4. Launching the Project

The project launch phase establishes the foundation for successful
delivery.

The course highlights:

-   defining the project's scope;
-   identifying stakeholders;
-   understanding stakeholder expectations;
-   gathering requirements;
-   creating a project charter;
-   forming agreements between the project team and sponsor/client;
-   preparing for the project's formal launch.

The project charter is particularly important because it provides a
shared understanding of the project's purpose, scope, expectations, and
authority before detailed execution begins.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 5. Planning the Project

Detailed project planning turns the charter and objectives into an
actionable delivery model.

Topics include:

-   breaking work into manageable components;
-   determining the work that needs to be done;
-   estimating time, cost, and scope;
-   developing the project schedule;
-   identifying risks;
-   creating a risk assessment;
-   planning how identified risks will be managed;
-   updating project plans as new information becomes available.

The course stresses that planning is not a one-time activity. Plans must
evolve as circumstances, risks, and knowledge change.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 6. Running the Project

Project execution requires active management rather than simply
following the original plan.

The course focuses on:

-   keeping the project running smoothly;
-   monitoring time, cost, and scope;
-   preventing or controlling scope creep;
-   managing issues and changes;
-   taking corrective action when the project moves off track;
-   properly closing the project;
-   capturing and transferring project knowledge for future projects.

The main principle is that successful project management involves
continuously comparing actual performance with the plan and intervening
when necessary.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 7. Agile and Hybrid Project Management

Not every project should be managed using a purely traditional approach.

This course introduces:

-   traditional / waterfall project management;
-   Agile project management;
-   hybrid approaches combining traditional and Agile methods;
-   situations in which different methodologies are appropriate;
-   the advantages of different project-management approaches.

The goal is not to treat one methodology as universally superior, but to
understand the available approaches and select or combine them according
to the characteristics of the project.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Communication and Influence

## 8. Unforgettable Storytelling

Business planning and project management depend on more than analysis.
Ideas also need to be communicated in a way that people can understand
and remember.

This course focuses on storytelling as a business communication skill,
including:

-   organizing information into a compelling narrative;
-   turning information, ideas, pitches, and proposals into stories;
-   cutting through information overload;
-   making messages memorable;
-   communicating in a way that audiences and prospective clients
    retain.

Storytelling therefore complements the analytical parts of the
collection: evidence may establish that an initiative is sound, while
effective communication helps stakeholders understand and support it.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# End-to-End Framework

Taken together, the collection describes an end-to-end path from idea to
delivery:

**Opportunity / Problem**\
↓\
**Business Case** --- Should we invest?\
↓\
**Business Plan** --- What are we trying to achieve and how?\
↓\
**Measures & KPIs** --- How will success be assessed?\
↓\
**Project Launch** --- Who is involved, what is the scope, and what are
the requirements?\
↓\
**Project Planning** --- What work, schedule, cost, resources, and risks
are involved?\
↓\
**Delivery Approach** --- Waterfall, Agile, or Hybrid\
↓\
**Project Execution & Control** --- Are time, cost, scope, and outcomes
on track?\
↓\
**Communication & Storytelling** --- Can stakeholders understand,
support, and act on the plan?\
↓\
**Project Closure & Knowledge Transfer**

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Key Takeaways

-   A **business case** establishes why an initiative is worth pursuing.
-   A **business plan** defines how the proposed initiative or business
    direction will be implemented.
-   Strong planning connects objectives, strategy, resources, decisions,
    and measurable outcomes.
-   **KPIs and other measures** provide evidence that implementation is
    producing the intended results.
-   A project needs clear **scope, stakeholders, requirements, and a
    project charter** before execution.
-   Detailed planning must address **time, cost, scope, schedule, and
    risk**.
-   Project plans are living artifacts and should change when
    circumstances change.
-   Project managers must actively control execution, especially **scope
    creep, changes, risks, and deviations from plan**.
-   **Waterfall, Agile, and Hybrid** approaches are alternatives that
    should be selected according to project needs rather than ideology.
-   Effective **storytelling and communication** are essential for
    gaining leadership and stakeholder buy-in.
-   Closing a project includes not only completing the work but also
    preserving lessons and knowledge for future initiatives.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Overall Learning Objective

The collection develops the ability to move systematically from a
business idea to an evidence-backed proposal, translate that proposal
into an executable project, manage delivery using an appropriate
methodology, measure results, and communicate the initiative
convincingly to decision-makers and stakeholders.

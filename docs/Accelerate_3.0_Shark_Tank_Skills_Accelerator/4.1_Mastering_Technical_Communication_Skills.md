## 2. Video: Planning Effective Technical Communications

### Key concept

-   Recognize the key considerations involved in planning technical
    communications.

Technology continues to expand into all facets of our lives, making
effective communication of technical content more critical than ever. In
this video, you'll learn about the key considerations involved in
planning technical communications, including determining the type and
format, defining the communication purpose, establishing measurable
goals, and selecting the optimal delivery method to achieve your desired
results.

Before you begin creating any technical content, there are several key
considerations to keep in mind. Regardless of role, everyone needs to
communicate technical topics at times, and how you plan that
communication makes the difference.

If you're discussing implementation of a new software application, begin
by identifying whether your technical content requires documentation
such as manuals or guides, visual aids like diagrams and infographics,
interactive formats including tutorials and demonstrations, or
multimedia presentations.

Take your audience's technical proficiency into account. Leverage
content-management systems like Confluence, Notion, and SharePoint,
along with structured authoring tools such as MadCap Flare and Adobe
FrameMaker to enable single-source publishing across multiple formats.
They support responsive design, ensuring consistency while optimizing
delivery for web, mobile, and print channels.

For example, a single-source approach allows you to create one
comprehensive troubleshooting guide that can be dynamically adapted for
both a detailed PDF manual and a mobile-friendly quick-reference guide.

Once you have determined your format, the next step is to define the
purpose of the communication. Clearly articulate what you want to
accomplish and distinguish between informational goals that build
understanding and instructional goals that drive action.

If users consistently abandon your troubleshooting guide at step three,
analytics reveal where your purpose and execution may be misaligned.
With a clearly defined purpose, you can now translate intent into
outcomes by setting measurable communication goals.

Rather than a vague goal like "improve user understanding," a measurable
goal might be "reduce average time to complete first software
configuration from thirty to twenty-five minutes within ninety days."
Contemporary approaches use SMART criteria: Specific, Measurable,
Achievable, Relevant, and Time-bound, alongside digital tools like Jira,
Productboard, and analytics dashboards for goal tracking.

Ensure goals reflect actual user contexts, pain points, and success
criteria throughout the content lifecycle using user personas created
through platforms like Xtensio and journey-mapping tools like Miro and
Smaply. Finally, select the delivery method to achieve desired results.
Align your chosen medium with how users prefer to learn and access
information in their workflow.

Consider the technical environment, accessibility requirements, and
whether communication needs to work across remote, hybrid, or in-person
scenarios. Communication channels may include adaptive content platforms
like Zoomin and Adobe Experience Manager that respond to user behavior
and AI-powered recommendation engines that serve the right content
format at the right moment for optimal engagement.

When you approach technical communication with intention and planning,
your message becomes clearer and more impactful. Understanding the key
considerations involved in planning technical communications provides a
structured foundation for effective decision-making.

## 4. Video: Analyzing Your Audience for Technical Topics

### Key concept

-   Recognize ways to analyze your audience to tailor your technical
    content to their expertise level and needs.

All effective communication relies on knowing your audience and adapting
your message to them. In this video, you'll learn about the different
ways you can analyze your audience, including conducting surveys and
questionnaires, meeting audience members, considering demographics,
assessing technical skill levels, and using this information to tailor
your technical content appropriately.

A brilliant technical document means nothing if it misses the mark with
the people who need to use it. Effective communication relies on knowing
your audience and adapting your message to them. Whether you are
explaining a complex system to senior executives or training new
employees on a software application, understanding your audience
determines how effective your message will be.

The first step is to analyze your audience using several proven methods.
One effective approach is to conduct surveys or questionnaires. These
gather specific information about your audience's knowledge,
expectations, and needs through multiple-choice questions, rating
scales, or open-ended responses.

Use digital survey tools like SurveyMonkey, Google Forms, Typeform, and
Qualtrics to gather data efficiently, and tools like MonkeyLearn and
Lexalytics for AI-powered sentiment analysis. It is a good idea to use
anonymous surveys for sensitive topics. For example, if respondents
indicate they have never used your software, the survey can skip
advanced feature questions and focus on baseline knowledge instead.

Another valuable technique is to meet audience members ahead of time.
Direct interactions provide qualitative insights that surveys may miss,
revealing concerns and organizational context. Connect with users
remotely and use Otter.ai for transcription and Dovetail for research
synthesis.

A third way to analyze your audience is to consider the demographics of
the audience. Survey results might reveal that staff struggling with an
AI system's features might not be captured by a survey. You should also
consider demographics such as job roles, departments, seniority levels,
and industry experience, which significantly influence how technical
information should be presented.

Use audience analytics tools including Google Analytics, LinkedIn
analytics, and platforms like Mixpanel and Amplitude for behavioral
data. For global audiences, tools like Tableau and Power BI can help
visualize demographic patterns for targeted communication. However,
avoid stereotypical thinking such as assuming that employees with longer
tenure are less comfortable with technology.

It is vital to remember that demographics provide context, not
conclusions.

Next, make sure to take time to assess technical skill levels. Determine
familiarity with domain-specific terminology and concepts through
pre-assessments using learning-management-system data from platforms
like Articulate and Moodle. Use adaptive testing platforms such as
Knowledge Smart to dynamically adjust question difficulty based on
responses.

Consider the audience's educational background, which may indicate
familiarity with basic database concepts. You can skip foundational
explanations and focus on advanced techniques.

Finally, use audience information thoughtfully. Employ journey-mapping
tools like Smaply and UXPressia to visualize pain points and align
content with actual workflows. Use feedback to understand what your
audience truly needs.

Understanding your audience transforms generic information into targeted
communication.

## 6. Video: Acquiring Subject Matter Knowledge Effectively

### Key concept

-   Identify the methods used to acquire thorough subject matter
    knowledge.

You may be called upon to communicate technical information on topics
where you lack expertise, but developing a working knowledge is
achievable. In this video, you'll learn about the methods for acquiring
thorough subject matter knowledge, including adopting a growth mindset,
learning anything that can be learned, conducting in-depth research from
websites and industry sources, deconstructing complex concepts into
smaller chunks, consulting with subject matter experts, and
participating in online learning communities.

There may be times when you're called upon to communicate technical
information on topics where you lack expertise. The key to doing this
successfully is developing enough expertise to discuss and present the
subject matter clearly and accurately. You don't need to become an
expert overnight, but you do need a working knowledge that allows you to
communicate authentically with the people involved.

The first step is to adopt a growth mindset. A growth mindset is the
belief that abilities and intelligence can be developed through
dedication and effort. It eliminates self-imposed barriers and
encourages curiosity and resilience in the face of complexity.

There are a host of online AI platforms like Coursera, LinkedIn
Learning, Udemy, and Pluralsight that enable continuous learning.

Conduct in-depth research. Thorough research involves systematically
exploring credible sources to gather foundational information,
understand terminology, and identify key concepts. Use AI-powered
research tools like Semantic Scholar, Elicit, and Perplexity AI.
Research-management tools like Zotero and Mendeley help organize
sources, while platforms like Notion and Obsidian enable knowledge
management.

As you gather information, deconstruct concepts into smaller chunks.
Breaking down complex technical concepts into manageable components
makes them easier to understand and explain. This technique is called
chunking. Use tools like Miro, MindMeister, and XMind to visualize
relationships between ideas.

Consult with subject matter experts to validate your understanding and
fill knowledge gaps. Ask specific questions and use spaced-repetition
software like Anki and Quizlet for long-term retention. AI tutors like
ChatGPT and Claude can be used for concept clarification and interactive
simulations on platforms like Brilliant.org to understand each
component.

Engage directly with experts who can provide authentic insights that go
beyond written materials. They can clarify nuances, share practical
applications, and validate your understanding. Connect with experts
through online platforms like LinkedIn, schedule informational
interviews, and attend virtual conferences on platforms like Hopin or
professional webinars.

Participate in specialized communities, including Stack Overflow for
programming and ResearchGate for academics. Use collaborative platforms
like Slack workspaces and Discord servers to maintain ongoing dialogues,
and book virtual office chats through Calendly.

Finally, participate in online learning communities. Engage with MOOCs
on Coursera, edX, and FutureLearn and participate in technical forums
like Stack Overflow, Dev.to, and Hashnode. Contribute to open-source
projects on GitHub and GitLab to learn through practical application,
and join peer-learning groups organized through Meetup.

By applying these methods, you build the foundation needed to
communicate about even unfamiliar technical topics with clarity and
credibility.

## 8. Video: Leveraging AI to Transform Technical Content

### Key concept

-   Recognize how AI tools can help communicate technical information to
    nontechnical audiences.

AI has revolutionized how communicators bridge the gap between technical
complexity and audience understanding. In this video, you'll learn how
to communicate technical information to nontechnical audiences with AI
tools, including simplifying complex technical concepts, translating
jargon into plain language, tailoring content to different expertise
levels, and creating relatable analogies and audience-appropriate
explanations.

AI has revolutionized how communicators bridge the gap between technical
complexity and audience understanding. AI-powered tools can help
transform dense content into clear, accessible communication, saving
time while ensuring quality. Whether you're creating documentation,
training materials, or executive summaries, AI can be a valuable partner
in the process of translating knowledge from technical experts to
diverse audiences.

For starters, you can simplify complex technical concepts with AI
assistance. You can paste a paragraph into ChatGPT, Claude, Google
Gemini, or Microsoft Copilot and ask it to explain it more simply or
identify which parts might confuse someone without a technical
background. Then review the output and vocabulary and suggest ways to
make it more accessible.

This is particularly useful when working with material outside your own
expertise and when you want to ensure your explanation makes sense. AI
can also help translate technical jargon into plain language. Every
field has specialized vocabulary, and what seems clear to experts can be
confusing to others. AI tools can identify jargon and suggest everyday
alternatives.

Sometimes you need to explain the same technical concept to different
audiences. AI can help tailor content for different expertise levels.
You can ask it to rewrite content for a new employee, a senior manager,
or executives who want the bottom line. AI tools can help create
multiple versions while preserving the core message.

Analogies connect unfamiliar concepts to things people already
understand, making abstract ideas concrete and technical content
memorable and easier to grasp. If you're struggling to explain how a
particular technology works, you can ask AI to suggest analogies. For
instance, it might explain cloud storage using the analogy of renting
space or compare a database to a library.

Another advantage of AI is that it can generate multiple options,
allowing you to choose the analogy that fits your audience best.
Finally, AI can help you create audience-appropriate explanations. This
goes beyond simplifying, crafting explanations that address specific
interests and concerns. Tools like Jasper AI, Copy.ai, and Writer.com
can help frame technical information in ways that resonate with specific
groups.

For a finance audience, you might emphasize cost savings and ROI. For an
operations team, you might focus on efficiency and workflow
improvements. AI can help identify these angles and adjust your
messaging accordingly.

AI tools offer powerful capabilities for technical communicators who
need to reach diverse audiences.

## 10. Video: Applying Key Principles of Technical Writing

### Key concept

-   Recognize strategies that you can use to apply the fundamental
    principles of technical writing.

Effective technical writing requires clarity and conciseness to ensure
your message translates well with any audience. In this video, you'll
learn how to apply the fundamental principles of technical writing,
including following the Simplified Technical English (STE) principles to
create reader-friendly documents, avoiding slang and jargon while using
approved technical terms, keeping sentences clear and concise, using
active voice to speak directly to your audience, formatting bulleted and
numbered lists for visual appeal and readability, and defining acronyms
and terms clearly.

Communicating technical information effectively requires techniques that
keep your writing clear and concise. It may seem natural to explain
technical concepts using technical language, but simplifying your
approach will help your message translate more clearly with any
audience. If you are unsure whether your writing is accessible, have
someone unfamiliar with the subject read through it to see if it makes
sense to them.

One foundational approach is to use Simplified Technical English
principles. STE is a controlled language framework designed to reduce
ambiguity and improve comprehension through a restricted vocabulary of
approved words, standardized grammar rules, and consistent sentence
structures.

Apply ASD-STE100 standards to ensure clarity across technical
documentation. Modern tools like Acrolinx, HyperSTE, and TechScribe
automatically check compliance with simplified English rules and suggest
improvements based on readability algorithms. These platforms integrate
directly with content-management systems like Confluence and SharePoint,
providing real-time feedback as writers create content.

Avoid slang and jargon. Use approved terminology and define terms on
first use. Modern approaches include maintaining dynamic glossaries in
content-management systems and implementing contextual tooltips using
tools like WalkMe and Whatfix to clarify terminology automatically. STE
also emphasizes one main idea per sentence and keeping sentences clear
and concise.

Use active voice where possible. Active voice clarifies responsibility,
improves readability, and reduces word count. Use readability-analysis
tools like Readable.io and Yoast SEO, and writing tools like Grammarly,
Hemingway Editor, and ProWritingAid for real-time feedback on sentence
complexity.

With technical communication, you want your audience to feel as if you
are speaking directly to them.

Ensure each sentence contains one main idea, in simple present tense
when possible, and avoid nested clauses.

Use readability-analysis tools for real-time feedback on sentence
complexity.

For technical communication, you want your audience to feel as if you
are speaking directly to them.

Use the active voice whenever possible. For example, instead of writing
"The total will be calculated by the system," write "The system
calculates the total." Active voice clarifies responsibility, improves
readability, and reduces word count.

Use formatting strategically. Microsoft Editor, Writer.com, and
PerfectIt can flag passive constructions. Visual organization matters,
so format with bulleted and numbered lists. Lists break complex
information into digestible chunks and improve scannability. Numbered
lists are best for sequential steps; bulleted lists present parallel
items. Use consistent punctuation and parallel structure to enhance
visual hierarchy.

Typora and Obsidian, or content-management systems like Notion and
Contentful, support responsive list formatting across devices.
Accessibility tools like WAVE ensure lists are properly structured for
screen readers.

Finally, always define terms and acronyms clearly. Implement automated
glossary systems in documentation platforms like MadCap Flare, RoboHelp,
and Paligo that link terms to definitions throughout your content.

Use hover-over definitions in digital content, and maintain centralized
terminology databases using tools like SDL MultiTerm and TermWeb. Keep
acronyms consistent throughout your content and spell them out on first
use.

Strategies for applying fundamental principles of technical writing
include using Simplified Technical English principles, avoiding slang
and jargon, keeping sentences clear and concise, using active voice,
formatting with bulleted and numbered lists, and defining terms and
acronyms clearly.

By applying these strategies, you turn complex content into accessible
communication.

## 12. Video: Enhancing Communication with Visual Aids

### Key concept

-   Identify the types of visual aids used for technical communication.

Visual and graphic aids can be just as important as the words themselves
in making complex information accessible. In this video, you'll learn
about the different types of visual aids used to enhance technical
communications, including when to use graphs and bar charts to show data
relationships and comparisons, how tables organize detailed information
for professional audiences, how flowcharts can visualize step-by-step
processes, how to apply pie charts to display proportional data and
percentages, and how to leverage photographs and images to illustrate
concepts and create powerful analogies.

Selecting from the types of visual aids for technical communication
depends on the information you are presenting and knowing when to use
each one helps you enhance your message effectively.

### Graphs and bar charts

Use graphs and bar charts for data relationships. Graphs and bar charts
transform numerical data into visual patterns that reveal trends,
correlations, and comparisons at a glance. Use graphs and bar charts
when you are presenting data and you know when to use each one helps you
enhance your message effectively.

Use graphs and bar charts for data relationships. Graphs and bar charts
transform numerical data into visual patterns that reveal trends,
correlations, and comparisons at a glance.

Use line graphs to show change over time.

### Tables

Tables organize complex information into structured rows and columns,
enabling systematic comparison of multiple variables. Design responsive
tables using web frameworks like Bootstrap and Tailwind CSS, and
data-visualization libraries including DataTables and AG Grid that
provide built-in sorting, filtering, and pagination.

Use tables for comparison of data elements.

### Flowcharts

Flowcharts organize process and workflow information. Use standard
symbols, clearly identify complex workflows, reveal dependencies, and
highlight bottlenecks. Modern tools include Lucidchart, Miro, Microsoft
Visio, and draw.io. These support real-time collaboration and version
control, or create clickable flowcharts with hyperlinked steps using
Figma and integration with process-automation platforms like Zapier.

Flowcharts for process visualization help map sequential steps, decision
points, and pathways within processes, transforming abstract procedures
into concrete visual representations.

### Pie charts

Pie charts show proportional data. They work best with limited
categories, typically five to seven segments, where showing relative
size matters more than precise values. Generate dynamic pie charts using
business-intelligence tools like Looker and Qlik, or modern charting
libraries such as Highcharts and Google Charts.

### Screenshots and images

Avoid 3D effects that distort perception, ensure sufficient
color-contrast ratios for accessibility, and implement responsive
images.

Screenshots and images bring tangible reality to technical
communications by showing actual equipment, interfaces, or results that
words alone cannot adequately convey. Visual aids transform technical

## Job Aid: Planning and Writing for Effective Technical Communication

### Planning Your Communication

When planning technical content, address these key considerations:

-   Determine the type and format:
    -   Documentation
    -   Visual aids
    -   Interactive formats
    -   Multimedia presentations
-   Define the purpose:
    -   Informational goals that build understanding
    -   Instructional goals that drive action
-   Establish measurable goals using SMART criteria:
    -   Specific
    -   Measurable
    -   Achievable
    -   Relevant
    -   Time-bound
-   Select the delivery method based on how users prefer to learn and
    access information.

### Analyzing Your Audience

Apply these techniques to understand your audience:

-   Conduct surveys or questionnaires
    -   Gather specific information about knowledge, expectations, and
        needs.
-   Meet audience members ahead of time
    -   Gain qualitative insights and reveal concerns.
-   Gather demographic data
    -   For example: job roles, departments, seniority levels, and
        industry experience.
-   Assess technical skill levels
    -   Use pre-assessments or conduct background research.
-   Use audience information thoughtfully
    -   Balance customization with clarity and avoid assumptions.

### Acquiring Subject Matter Knowledge

Build expertise on unfamiliar topics by:

-   Adopting a growth mindset
    -   Abilities can be developed through dedication and effort.
-   Conducting in-depth research
    -   Use credible sources, such as industry publications and academic
        journals.
-   Deconstructing concepts into smaller chunks
    -   Easier to understand and explain.
-   Consulting with subject matter experts
    -   Gather authentic insights and learn about practical
        applications.
-   Participating in online learning
    -   Look for and join communities, forums, and professional
        networks.

### Leveraging AI for Technical Communication

AI tools can help transform technical content by:

-   Simplifying complex concepts
    -   Analyzes structure and suggests more accessible explanations.
-   Translating jargon into plain language
    -   Makes dense text more readable using everyday vocabulary.
-   Adapting content for different expertise levels
    -   Tailors depth through expert guidance.
-   Generating relatable analogies
    -   Helps connect unfamiliar concepts to familiar ideas, making them
        easier to comprehend.
-   Creating audience-appropriate explanations
    -   Generates material tailored to specific interests and concerns.

### Applying Technical Writing Principles

Follow these fundamental principles for clear writing:

-   Use Simplified Technical English (STE) principles
    -   Use restricted vocabulary and standardized grammar.
-   Avoid slang and jargon
    -   Use precise, approved terminology and define terms on first use.
-   Keep sentences clear and concise
    -   Follow the KISS principle (Keep It Simple and Straightforward).
-   Use active voice
    -   Change passive voice to active voice wherever possible.
-   Format with lists
    -   Use bulleted lists for parallel items.
    -   Use numbered lists for sequential steps.
-   Define terms and acronyms clearly upon first use
    -   Spell out acronyms in full on first mention, followed by the
        abbreviation in parentheses.

# Expert Reflections: Perfecting Your Elevator Pitch

**Expert:** Suzanne Knight\
**Topic:** Perfecting Your Elevator Pitch

## Course Overview

This expert reflection focuses on creating an elevator pitch that is
**authentic, clear, and compelling**.

An elevator pitch is a short introduction---typically around **30
seconds to one minute**---that can be used to introduce yourself, make
an ask, explain what you are passionate about, describe what you are
working on, and communicate the value you bring.

The objective is not to compress an entire résumé into a few sentences.
A strong elevator pitch selects the most relevant information and
presents it in a way that makes the listener want to continue the
conversation.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 1. Know Your Audience

The first step is to consider who will hear the pitch.

The same pitch should not necessarily be used for every audience. What
matters to a senior leader may differ from what matters to a new
colleague, prospective employer, client, or collaborator.

Before preparing the pitch, ask:

-   Who am I speaking to?
-   What are they likely to care about?
-   What information will be relevant to them?
-   What do I want them to remember about me?
-   What do I want to happen after the conversation?

The pitch should remain authentic while adjusting its emphasis to the
audience.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 2. Identify What You Are Passionate About

A compelling pitch communicates more than a job title.

Think about:

-   what you enjoy doing;
-   what gives you energy;
-   what kinds of problems you like solving;
-   what you are currently working on;
-   what you would like to do more of.

Passion makes the pitch more memorable, but it should be expressed
naturally rather than exaggerated.

A useful question is:

> What do I genuinely want this person to understand about what
> motivates me?

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 3. Explain the Value You Bring

An elevator pitch should answer an implicit question:

> Why should the listener care?

Instead of simply describing responsibilities, communicate the value
created by your work.

For example:

``` text
Responsibility:
I work on automation projects.

Value:
I build automation solutions that reduce repetitive manual work
and allow teams to spend more time on higher-value activities.
```

Think about value in terms of:

-   problems solved;
-   improvements created;
-   time saved;
-   costs reduced;
-   revenue generated;
-   risks reduced;
-   customer or employee experience improved;
-   collaboration enabled.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 4. Use Concrete Examples

Specific evidence makes a pitch more credible.

Rather than relying on broad claims such as:

> I am a strong problem solver.

provide an example that demonstrates the capability.

A useful structure is:

``` text
Capability
    ↓
Example
    ↓
Impact
```

For instance:

``` text
I enjoy simplifying complex technical processes.
Recently, I helped redesign...
As a result...
```

Examples can come from your current role, previous jobs, projects,
volunteering, or other relevant experiences.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 5. Quantify Impact Where Possible

Numbers can make achievements easier to understand.

Useful metrics may include:

-   revenue;
-   cost savings;
-   time saved;
-   productivity improvements;
-   number of users;
-   project scale;
-   number of teams or countries involved;
-   reduction in incidents or errors.

For example:

``` text
Less specific:
I helped improve the process.

More specific:
I redesigned part of the process, reducing average handling time by 30%.
```

Not every accomplishment needs a number. Use metrics when they genuinely
clarify the scale or impact of the work.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 6. Look Beyond Your Formal Role

Your strongest evidence may not always come from your official job
responsibilities.

Consider experiences such as:

-   previous roles;
-   cross-functional initiatives;
-   volunteering;
-   community activities;
-   side projects;
-   mentoring;
-   professional interests.

These experiences may reveal capabilities such as:

-   leadership;
-   communication;
-   initiative;
-   creativity;
-   collaboration;
-   resilience;
-   problem solving.

The goal is to identify examples that strengthen the professional story
you want to communicate.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 7. Keep the Pitch Focused

A short pitch cannot communicate everything.

Prioritize the most important ideas and remove unnecessary detail.

A practical structure is:

``` text
Who I am
    ↓
What I do / care about
    ↓
The value I create
    ↓
Evidence
    ↓
What I want next
```

The pitch should create enough interest for the listener to ask another
question.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 8. Speak Naturally

An elevator pitch should sound like spoken language, not a résumé being
read aloud.

Avoid:

-   long lists of responsibilities;
-   excessive corporate language;
-   unnecessary jargon;
-   complicated sentence structures;
-   memorizing every word so rigidly that the delivery sounds
    artificial.

Prefer:

-   short sentences;
-   familiar vocabulary;
-   concrete examples;
-   natural transitions;
-   a conversational rhythm.

Practice the structure and key ideas rather than trying to reproduce
every word perfectly.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 9. Bring Appropriate Energy

Delivery influences how the message is received.

Consider:

-   pace;
-   tone;
-   confidence;
-   emphasis;
-   body language;
-   enthusiasm.

If the subject genuinely matters to you, allow that interest to be
visible.

At the same time, avoid forcing enthusiasm. Authenticity is more
persuasive than exaggerated energy.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 10. Practice and Refine

A strong elevator pitch usually requires iteration.

Practice it aloud and evaluate:

-   Does it sound like me?
-   Is the main message obvious?
-   Is it too long?
-   Are there unnecessary details?
-   Is the value clear?
-   Is the example specific enough?
-   Does the ending create a natural next step?

Continue refining until the pitch can be delivered confidently without
sounding memorized.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# A Practical Elevator Pitch Framework

A reusable structure is:

## 1. Identity

Who are you?

``` text
I'm a [role / professional identity] focused on...
```

## 2. Focus

What are you particularly interested in?

``` text
I'm especially interested in...
```

## 3. Value

What value do you create?

``` text
I help [audience/team] achieve [outcome] by...
```

## 4. Evidence

Provide one concise example.

``` text
For example, recently I...
```

## 5. Direction or Ask

Explain what you want next when appropriate.

``` text
I'm now looking to...
```

Combined:

``` text
I'm a [professional identity] focused on [area].
I'm particularly interested in [problem/domain].
I help [stakeholders] achieve [value].
For example, I recently [specific example + impact].
I'm now looking to [direction / opportunity / ask].
```

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Example: Software / AI Engineer

``` text
I'm an AI software engineer focused on turning generative AI
capabilities into practical enterprise applications.

I particularly enjoy solving problems where complex internal
knowledge or workflows make it difficult for people to get
reliable answers quickly.

Recently, I've been working on AI applications that combine
software engineering, enterprise data, and LLM-based workflows
to make those processes more efficient and usable.

I'm especially interested in opportunities where I can take AI
products from experimentation into reliable production systems.
```

This example works because it communicates:

-   professional identity;
-   area of specialization;
-   motivation;
-   value;
-   relevant experience;
-   future direction.

It does not attempt to list every technology or project.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 30-Second Version

When time is limited:

``` text
I'm an AI software engineer focused on building practical
enterprise applications with generative AI. I enjoy turning
complex knowledge and workflows into tools that make it easier
for people to find reliable information and get work done.
My main interest is taking AI ideas beyond prototypes and
turning them into reliable production solutions.
```

# Audience-Specific Adaptation

The same core story can be adjusted depending on the listener.

## Senior Leader

Emphasize:

-   business impact;
-   strategic relevance;
-   measurable outcomes;
-   scale.

## Hiring Manager

Emphasize:

-   expertise;
-   relevant experience;
-   accomplishments;
-   the type of role you want.

## Technical Leader

Emphasize:

-   engineering depth;
-   architecture;
-   production experience;
-   difficult technical problems solved.

## New Colleague

Emphasize:

-   what you work on;
-   how your responsibilities connect;
-   opportunities to collaborate.

## Networking Contact

Emphasize:

-   professional identity;
-   interests;
-   distinctive experience;
-   what you want to learn or explore.

# Preparation Worksheet

Who will hear this pitch?

### Objective

What do I want to happen after the pitch?

### Professional Identity

How do I describe what I do in one sentence?

### Passion

What type of work genuinely interests me?

### Value

What useful outcomes do I create?

### Evidence

What is one strong example that proves this?

### Metrics

Can I quantify the impact?

### Differentiator

What makes my experience or perspective distinctive?

### Ask

What do I want from the listener?

# Final Checklist

Before using your elevator pitch:

-   [ ] It can be delivered in approximately 30--60 seconds.
-   [ ] It is tailored to the audience.
-   [ ] It clearly communicates who I am.
-   [ ] It explains what I care about or specialize in.
-   [ ] It communicates value rather than only responsibilities.
-   [ ] It contains a concrete example when appropriate.
-   [ ] It uses evidence or metrics where useful.
-   [ ] It avoids unnecessary jargon.
-   [ ] It sounds natural when spoken aloud.
-   [ ] It communicates genuine energy and interest.
-   [ ] It ends with a clear direction or ask when appropriate.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Key Takeaways

1.  **An elevator pitch is a focused professional story, not a
    compressed résumé.**
2.  **Start with the audience and decide what is most relevant to
    them.**
3.  **Communicate what you are passionate about and what value you
    bring.**
4.  **Use specific examples to make claims credible.**
5.  **Quantify achievements when numbers make the impact clearer.**
6.  **Look beyond your current job description for evidence of
    transferable strengths.**
7.  **Keep the pitch concise enough to invite further conversation.**
8.  **Use natural language and avoid sounding over-rehearsed.**
9.  **Energy and authenticity are important parts of delivery.**
10. **Practice, test, and continuously refine the pitch.**

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Final Mental Model

``` text
Know the audience
       ↓
Choose the message
       ↓
Express your passion
       ↓
Explain your value
       ↓
Support it with evidence
       ↓
Keep it concise
       ↓
Deliver it authentically
       ↓

```

The purpose of an elevator pitch is not to tell someone everything about
you. It is to make the **right information memorable enough that they
want to know more**.
